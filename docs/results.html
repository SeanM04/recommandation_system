Results · Phase 4

What learning in real time is worth

Every configured policy served the same simulated customers, opening the app at the same moments, and was scored against an oracle that knows the true take-up probabilities. Halfway through, customer behaviour changed.

Findings

Measured
  1. Learning per segment beats both baselines, in every run. thompson_segment_decay reached 92.7% of the oracle, with 53% more take-ups per app open than the static batch model (0.263 against 0.172). It cut regret by 85% against random and 81% against static: below random's regret in 10 of 10 seeds and below static's in 10 of 10.
  2. It adapts when behaviour changes; the batch model does not. After the shift its take-up rose from 0.231 to 0.295 per app open; the static model went from 0.168 to 0.175.
  3. The top of the table is a tie. thompson_segment_decay has the lowest mean regret, but thompson_segment, thompson_segment_payday had lower regret in at least a fifth of the seeds, so the order among them is within the noise.
  4. Personalisation is the biggest single lever. Thompson sampling with statistics per segment reached 92.4% of the oracle; one global model reached 67.7% (lower regret per segment in 10 of 10 seeds).
  5. Forgetting trades a little before the shift for more after it. With forgetting, regret after the shift was lower in 10 of 10 seeds, by 130 take-ups on average; before the shift it cost 93 on average (higher in 8 of 10 seeds). Its value grows with how often, and how sharply, behaviour changes.
  6. The reward decides the business outcome. The same policy learning from margin instead of take-up earned 38% more revenue per app open (R13.37 against R9.68, more in 10 of 10 seeds), at the cost of 22% fewer take-ups. Which one is right is a business choice, not a modelling one.
  7. Thompson sampling explores better than epsilon-greedy. With the same per-segment statistics, regret was 1,078 against 1,690, lower in 8 of 10 seeds: epsilon-greedy keeps spending a fixed share of slots on offers it already knows are poor.
  8. Forgetting adapts sooner. After the shift, with forgetting the young professionals' new favourite led within 1,460 app opens, against 5,601 app opens without; for families, 7,980 app opens against not within the run. The static model adapted for neither segment (first seed; see the adaptation table).

Regret over time

A learning policy's curve bends and flattens as it finds the right offers: each app open adds less regret than the one before. A straight line means the policy keeps making the same mistakes. Watch the slope after the shift.

Method

  • The same app opens for everyone. Arrivals (which customer, which day, which time of day) come from their own random stream, so every policy faces the identical sequence. Customer responses use a second stream.
  • An oracle for scoring. For every app open, the oracle ranks the eligible offers by their true probability. Regret is the oracle's expected take-ups minus the policy's expected take-ups for the list it actually showed, using the response model. Expected values measure the choice, not the luck of one draw.
  • A fair batch baseline. The static policy is fitted on a warm-up month of 5,000 app opens gathered by a random policy before the experiment, then frozen. The learning policies start with no data at all.
  • The behaviour shift. Halfway through, streaming becomes the young professionals' favourite and electricity the families'. See the simulated world.
  • Ten seeds, compared in pairs. The whole comparison runs on ten seeds and the table reports the mean and spread. Within a seed every policy sees the same app opens, so two policies can be compared seed by seed: each finding says in how many seeds a difference holds, and calls a tie a tie. The chart shows the first seed.
  • Two objectives. Most policies learn from take-ups. thompson_segment_revenue learns from margin, so it is judged by revenue per app open rather than by take-up regret.

Reproduce

make compare      # every policy, 10 seeds, 50,000 app opens each (about 7 minutes)
make docs         # rebuild this page from docs/data/comparison.json
make simulate     # one policy, fast mode: 50,000 app opens in about 5 seconds
python scripts/run_simulation.py --mode api      # the same loop through the running API
python scripts/run_simulation.py --persist       # save the learned model to the database
make experiment   # the focused forgetting experiment behind decision P9

Full outputs land in results/: the headline table as CSV and Markdown, one row per policy and seed, every app open of the first seed as Parquet, and the learning policies' beliefs over time for the dashboard.

Everything on this page is generated from docs/data/comparison.json, written by make compare.
